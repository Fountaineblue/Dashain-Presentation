01 / WHAT IS DASHAIN?

MORE THAN
A FESTIVAL.

Dashain (दशैं) is one of Nepal's most important festivals, celebrated for 15 days, usually in September or October.

It's a time of family reunions, blessings, worship, celebration, and delicious food.

But ask many Nepalis what Dashain truly means, and the answer isn't about rituals alone.

It's about coming home.

02 / THE STORY BEHIND THE FESTIVAL

GOOD WINS.
HOPE STAYS.

At the heart of Dashain is the celebration of the victory of good over evil.

In an important Hindu tradition, Goddess Durga defeats the demon Mahishasura, symbolizing the triumph of divine strength.

Other traditions also associate Vijayadashami with Lord Rama's victory over Ravana.

Strength. Courage. Renewal.

04 / THE HEART OF DASHAIN

A LITTLE RED.
A LOT OF LOVE.

On Vijayadashami, younger members of the family receive blessings from their elders.

Tika: Often a red mixture of rice, yogurt, and vermilion placed on the forehead.

Jamara: Sacred yellow-green sprouts grown from the first day of Dashain.

Dakshina: Money sometimes gifted along with blessings. A childhood favorite!

Love, passed down through generations.

05 / A POSTCARD FROM OLD KATHMANDU

THE SKY WAS
FULL OF KITES.

Imagine Kathmandu in the 1980s.

Children flying changa from rooftops, bamboo swings called linge ping, festive clothes, and the sound of old transistor radios.

School holidays. Visiting relatives. Collecting dakshina. Trying to cut another kite's string in the sky.

The kind of memories that stay forever.

07 / TRADITION THROUGH TIME

TRADITIONS
TRAVEL.

For some, Dashain means the journey back to their ancestral village or family home.

For others, it means recreating the same traditions thousands of kilometers away.

Today, Nepalis around the world celebrate through gatherings, homemade food, video calls, and blessings.

Home isn't always a place. Sometimes, it's people.
